What is takuhaibin?

Japan's hotel-to-hotel luggage forwarding is genuinely mainstream — locals use it constantly, and with a transit-only trip like yours it's close to essential rather than a splurge. You hand your bag to a courier (almost always Yamato Transport, branded "kuroneko" / black cat) at your hotel's front desk in the morning, and it shows up at your next hotel by the following day. You carry only a day bag while it travels separately.

Leg 1 — Tokyo → Osaka

Drop off: Morning of Day 7, at the JW Marriott Tokyo bell desk.
Arrives: Osaka Station Hotel, typically by early evening the same day or the following morning — confirm the same-day cutoff time when you drop it off (usually needs to be handed over by mid-morning).
Cost: ~¥2,000–3,000 per bag (~$14–20) — roughly $30–40 for two large bags.

Leg 2 — Osaka → Tokyo

Drop off: Morning of Day 11, at the Osaka Station Hotel front desk.
Arrives: Tokyo Marriott Hotel (Shinagawa) — your final-night hotel, a different property from where you started — typically next-morning rather than same-day for this direction. Since you're staying that extra night now, this is no longer time pressure like it would've been on a same-day departure.
Cost: Same ~$14–20 per bag, ~$30–40 for two.

Day 12 — no forwarding needed

Since you're already at the Tokyo Marriott Hotel the night before your flight, the final hop to Haneda is just a short Keikyu Line ride or taxi — carry your bags yourselves that morning rather than paying to forward them one more time. This is the one leg of the trip where forwarding isn't worth it.

Good to know before you go

  1. Book at the front desk, or a convenience store. Upscale hotels like the JW Marriott and Osaka Station Hotel typically handle takuhaibin directly at the concierge or bell desk. If a hotel doesn't, any 7-Eleven, Family Mart, or Lawson can process a Yamato drop-off — just ask for "takkyubin" (宅急便).
  2. Same-day vs. next-day matters. Same-day delivery (bag arrives that evening) usually requires drop-off by mid-morning, often around 10–11am. Miss that window and it becomes next-day. Plan your Day 7 and Day 11 mornings with this in mind if you want your bags waiting when you arrive.
  3. The Shinkansen oversized-baggage rule. On the Tokaido/Sanyo line (your Tokyo↔Osaka route), any bag with total dimensions (height + width + depth) over 160cm technically needs a free advance seat reservation in the space behind the last row, or you risk a fee if you're carrying it yourself. Forwarding sidesteps this entirely — one more reason to use it rather than wheel large suitcases onto the train.
  4. Cash helps. Some hotel-desk and convenience-store takuhaibin counters are cash-preferred for the courier fee, so keep enough yen on hand for both legs (~¥6,000–8,000 total for two bags across both drop-offs is a safe cushion).
  5. Station lockers are a different tool. Coin lockers (¥300–700) are everywhere and great for day-trip storage (e.g., stashing bags while you explore Kyoto or Nara for the day), but they don't move your luggage between cities — that's what forwarding is for.
  6. Pack a real day bag. Since your main luggage travels separately and you won't see it again until the next hotel, keep a backpack with a change of clothes, chargers, medication, and anything you need same-day — sized to fit under a train seat, since you'll be carrying it through every transit-heavy day.
  7. Label everything. Hotels will ask for your name and next hotel's address — having the Osaka Station Hotel and Tokyo Marriott Hotel confirmation details handy (address, phone number, check-in date) speeds this up at both counters.

Currency & staying connected

Money

Japan is more cash-reliant than the US, especially at temples, small restaurants, and takuhaibin counters. 7-Eleven ATMs are the most reliable for foreign card withdrawals. Tipping isn't customary and can cause confusion if offered. Notify your card companies of your travel dates before you leave.

Connectivity

An eSIM (Airalo, Ubigi, or similar) is the easiest option — install before you leave, activate on arrival. Pocket WiFi rental is a solid alternative if you'd rather share one connection. Don't rely on free hotel/station WiFi for navigation — it's inconsistent.

Electrical & time zone

Japan runs on 100V, Type A plugs (same shape as the US) — most electronics work without a converter. Japan is 13 hours ahead of Eastern Time (14 during US Daylight Saving, since Japan doesn't observe DST).
